What each layer is allowed to do

The constraint that keeps this honest: business rules never touch Prisma, and controllers never re-implement a rule. A verdict has exactly one source.

  1. 01routes/ Mount paths, attach authenticate and requireRole. No logic.
  2. 02controllers/ Validate input with Zod, load records, shape the response. Orchestration only.
  3. 03services/ Multi-step transactions that must not diverge — pricing recalculation and approval routing.
  4. 04rules/ Pure decisions: ceiling resolution, blended risk scoring, warehouse selection, proration. Deterministic, no I/O.
  5. 05prisma/ The 36-model schema. Snapshot columns freeze the ceiling and cost that applied at write time.

Deliverable §8 · What we would build next

Ranked by what the demo already exposes

Ordered by how visible the gap is to someone using the product, not by how interesting it is to build. The first two are things a careful reviewer would notice inside five minutes.

  1. 01

    Deliver the nudge, don't just record it

    Debt

    A nudge writes a real DEAL_NUDGE_SENT row to the audit ledger with actor, target rep and days-inactive — but the rep only sees it if they open the dashboard. There is no notification model, so the next step is an in-app inbox plus email, with the existing 12-hour cooldown as the send policy.

  2. 02

    Per-category discount baselines

    Accuracy

    The anomaly detector compares a quote against the rep's own historical average across all settled deals. A rep who sells mostly thin-margin services has a legitimately higher baseline than one selling hardware, so the honest next cut is a baseline per rep per category, falling back to the blended figure below the sample threshold.

  3. 03

    Multi-currency on price lists

    Spec bonus

    PriceList.currency already exists and defaults to INR, but every total is summed as if one currency. Real support means an FX rate table, a currency snapshot on the quotation, and money formatting driven by the quote rather than the locale.

  4. 04

    Product variants through the whole flow

    Debt

    ProductVariant is modelled and QuotationLine.productVariantId is wired, but the builder and the inventory allocator both reason at product level. Variants need their own stock rows before "Size / Pack" means anything at fulfillment time.

  5. 05

    API-level integration tests in CI

    Debt

    Four rule fixtures cover scoring and routing, and there are standalone portal, negotiation and invoicing scripts — but nothing runs them together on a push. The valuable suite is the §9 walkthrough itself, driven end to end against a seeded database.

  6. 06

    Push the approval queue instead of polling

    Scale

    Deal health and the approvals queue both refetch on an interval. With more than a handful of concurrent reviewers that is the first thing to strain; the queue is a natural fit for server-sent events keyed on approval-step changes.
